# Phase 4 editorial workflow design

Status: Proposed for owner review. This document defines Task 1 decisions and identifies client decisions that still gate implementation. It does not claim any Phase 4 operation is implemented.

## Intent and scope

Approved members should take an owned article from a private draft to immediate publication, keep it accurate, and archive it. Administrators should manage all articles and the publication's people, taxonomy, curation, settings, and audit history. The existing [product requirements](../../product-requirements.md), [API contracts](../../api-contracts.md), [data model](../../data-model.md), and [security rules](../../security-and-permissions.md) remain authoritative for MVP scope, roles, and stored constraints.

Phase 4 supplies editorial storage and dashboard controls. Public reader routes, published search, public media rendering, and public slug redirects are Phase 5 work. Scheduled publication, comments, newsletter integration, legacy import, and public letter submission remain deferred. Synthetic fixtures do not settle real category names, site copy, or institutional policy.

## Approaches considered

1. **Narrow, audited operations (recommended):** Extend the existing checked draft pattern with operation-specific commands and database guards. Each state change derives its actor from Auth, uses an expected version and request ID, and commits article/links/history together. This costs more small functions but makes authorization, replay, and audit behavior testable per operation.
2. **Direct administrator table writes:** Reuse existing membership RLS and grant similar writes for more tables. This is shorter initially, but cannot guarantee one trusted audit event, revision, and idempotent result for every write. It also differs from the accepted article boundary.
3. **One generic editorial mutation:** Route all domains through a single privileged function. This reduces entry points but creates a broad authority surface and makes field and action allowlists harder to review.

The recommended approach preserves Phase 3's user-scoped client, closed article writes, strict validation, and atomic history. The owner must approve this proposed choice before implementation. Existing direct administrator membership grants are a foundation exception that Task 9 must explicitly transition if this approach is accepted.

## Authorization and lifecycle

- Trusted Google identity, confirmed `bc.edu` email, active linked profile, and active membership are checked at each server action and again in the database operation. A credited writer gains no owner authority. An eligible administrator may act across articles; ordinary authors may act only on owned articles.
- Proposed allowed transitions: `draft -> published` by owner/admin; `published -> archived` by owner/admin; `archived -> published` by administrator restore when the archived record remains publication-ready. Restore keeps the original `published_at` and clears `archived_at`. No direct `archived -> draft` transition, schedule, or implicit status change during a content save.
- Owner decision (2026-11-01): owners and administrators may edit drafts and published articles; archived articles are read-only until an administrator restores them. An ordinary edit of published content keeps its status and original `published_at`. Its current edit version advances only for a changed save.
- **Owner decision (2026-11-01):** article slugs are never manually editable. A draft slug is generated from its title and follows title changes until first publication; after publication it stays fixed even if the title changes. Contributor author-page slugs follow display-name changes until the profile first owns or is credited on a published article, then stay fixed. Editors change titles/display names, not slugs. Existing stored slugs are preserved by migration, and future public URL changes require a separate redirect contract.
- Publish must reject an unsupported or empty canonical body, inactive/invalid selected references, and a missing or invalid main image. The MVP article view requires a main graphic and credit; the image also needs alt text for accessibility. Existing optional draft SEO fields remain optional unless the owner approves a stricter publication rule. Publication readiness is an invariant of every committed `published` row. Publish, a changed published edit, and administrator restore each recheck the supported body, active references, and finalized main image after locks. An old archived row that fails this check stays archived and requires a separately reviewed repair path; restoration must not silently publish incomplete content. The form check is advisory; the database check is authoritative.
- A transition command is proposed as `{requestId, articleId, expectedVersion}`; the server does not accept owner, actor, status, timestamps, or audit fields from the client. Direct callable database operations independently recheck eligibility and state. Errors use the accepted safe `invalid_input`, `not_found`, `forbidden`, `conflict`, `request_conflict`, and `unavailable` style where applicable; cross-owner and missing IDs stay indistinguishable.
- Receipt identity is actor plus request UUID and operation/payload fingerprint. An exact authorized replay returns the committed result even after later changes; a mismatched replay fails. Changed transitions increment `edit_version` once and capture one complete revision and one allowlisted audit event in the same transaction. Failed, stale, unauthorized, and exact replayed transitions capture no extra history. Lock ordering and post-wait checks must protect against concurrent saves, publication, deactivation, and reference changes.

## Generated slug transition

The current Phase 3 draft form exposes a slug field, and current draft RPCs require a caller-supplied slug. Task 2 removes that authoring control and makes the trusted database operation derive the canonical stored slug; hiding the field alone would leave direct RPC customization. The server action accepts only title/content fields, while the database independently derives or verifies the generated value. Existing published rows and their slugs are not rewritten. Existing draft rows keep their current slug until a successful save, which derives it from the current title.

Proposed slug normalization uses Unicode NFKD for Latin accents, lowercase ASCII letters/numbers, collapsed single hyphens, and a deterministic `article`/`author` fallback when no ASCII word remains. A transaction-safe numeric suffix resolves collisions without asking editors to invent a slug. Task 2's focused plan must prove stable replay, concurrent same-title creates, length bounds, and no overwrite of another article. The exact normalizer and collision limit are technical choices to review in Task 1 before implementation.

Current foundation profile grants allow authenticated users to update `slug` directly. Later contributor and own-profile tasks must remove that direct customization and derive prepublication profile slugs from display name through checked operations. Until then, the contract does not claim profile slug immutability is enforced.

## Media and rich content

- Proposed upload intake limits: JPEG, PNG, or WebP verified from decoded bytes; at most 10 MiB input, at most 6,000 pixels on either axis, and at most 24 million decoded pixels. Re-encode to a safe raster output with unneeded metadata removed where practical. These numeric limits are a technical proposal for owner review, not a current policy.
- Storage object keys are generated, not derived from filenames. Upload/request and finalization are separate checked steps; only a finalized valid asset can be attached. Database and Storage policies enforce uploader ownership with administrator override. Failed uploads remain untrusted and may be cleaned up only when unreferenced. Removal of any retained article's media is forbidden.
- The article main image reference, credit, and alt text are stored as validated metadata. Article-to-asset references, including body images, are captured transactionally in relational storage so retention checks do not depend on searching free-form JSON. Rich body images reference finalized assets by ID and have bounded alt/caption fields; arbitrary source URLs, HTML, SVG, and data URLs are rejected. Existing Phase 3 body documents and revisions stay readable and unchanged after additive grammar support.
- Approved video embeds use a strict provider and URL allowlist, safe rendering, and no pasted iframe HTML. **Client decision pending:** name the permitted providers before enabling video embeds. The editorial control for video stays unavailable until approved.
- The rich-content extension is an independent Task 4 between secure media storage and published editing. It covers the versioned validator, editor adapter, safe private preview, body image/caption controls, and approved video only after its provider choice. Public rendering remains Phase 5.

## Administrator operations

- Article lists and audit history use bounded pagination, independent admin guards, and safe summary fields. Restore uses the lifecycle command/version rule above. Audit actors come from trusted Auth, never a submitted field.
- Membership operations approve, change role, or deactivate; they do not create/delete Auth identities or change Google eligibility. Owner decision (2026-11-01): an administrator may not deactivate or demote the last eligible active administrator. The database locks/checks this invariant in the mutation transaction, including concurrent requests. Same-user self-deactivation is permitted only if another eligible active administrator remains. The exact lock and error contract remains for Task 1 review.
- The foundation currently grants authenticated direct membership writes under administrator RLS. To make membership changes auditable, Task 9 must add checked operations and revoke direct application mutation grants/policies in a reviewed forward migration before its UI is exposed. No existing dashboard membership writer exists, but compatibility and role denial tests must prove the transition. The user-scoped client remains unchanged.
- Guest contributor profiles remain separate from login identities. Profile editing never accepts `auth_user_id` or role. **Owner decision (2026-11-01):** an author-page slug becomes fixed once the profile owns or is credited on a published article; earlier changes must still obey uniqueness. Sections/categories retain their existing active states; tags currently have no active state. Tag creation/renaming waits for an accepted Unicode/punctuation normalization rule. **Owner decision (2026-11-01):** an administrator cannot deactivate a section, category, or contributor profile referenced by a published article. To preserve the accepted restore-before-edit flow, this proposed contract also blocks deactivation while an archived article still references it; drafts may retain an unavailable choice for deliberate repair. Referenced records are not cascade-deleted.
- Homepage selections accept published, non-archived articles and use deterministic recent-content fallbacks. Site settings accept only reviewed mission/contact/social/submission fields; unprovided real values remain unset. Each domain adds only allowlisted audit actions and resource types with no article body, private identity link, credentials, or arbitrary payload.
- Permanent deletion remains gated on the client's retention answer and an approved treatment of restrictive revision FKs, retained audit IDs, actor Auth references, and unreferenced media. No provisional delete operation or UI is enabled.

## Review and validation boundary

Before implementation, the owner reviews the proposed lifecycle transitions, main-image readiness, numeric image limits, and narrow mutation approach. Published article/profile slug, archived-edit, last-administrator, and published-reference deactivation choices are recorded above. The published-slug choice is resolved above; provider names, tag normalization, site values, real taxonomy labels, retention, and BC policies remain explicit follow-ups.

Each implementation task receives exact files/signatures, a failing focused test, a passing focused test, full affected checks, a small commit sequence, and a reviewer checkpoint in the [Phase 4 plan](../plans/2026-10-29-phase-four-editorial-controls.md). Database tests must cover owner/admin/credited non-owner/inactive/anonymous calls, direct grants, races, replay, rollback, and original-row preservation. Browser checks cover keyboard, mobile, loading, empty, error, and unsaved states. Human security/accessibility review and hosted provider/backup exercises remain separately reported until performed.
